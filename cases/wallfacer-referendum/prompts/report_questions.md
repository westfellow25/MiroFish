# ReportAgent Prompts — Deep Interaction Questions

After the simulation completes, interact with the ReportAgent (Step 4) and with individual agents (Step 5 — Deep Interaction) using these prompts. Run them in order; each question builds on the prior context.

## Part A — Macro-Structural Questions (to ReportAgent)

1. Summarize the distribution of the ten discourse factions at the end of the simulation window (Day 90). Compare with the initial distribution at Day 3 shown in the seed corpus. Which factions grew, which shrank, which emerged as unforeseen, and which collapsed.

2. Identify the three most consequential tipping points during the simulation. For each, describe the precipitating event, the cascade of propagation (from which actor to which communities), and the irreversibility test: if the event had not occurred, would the trajectory have been significantly different.

3. Assess the probability distribution over the UN Security Council vote outcomes (pass / fail / postponed / substantially modified). Root each probability in the institutional positions of the P5 and the rotating members as they evolved during the simulation.

4. Identify which national ratification processes would likely succeed and which would fail. Distinguish between constitutional majorities, parliamentary majorities, and public referendum majorities — these may diverge.

5. List institutional actors that emerged during the simulation but were not present in the seed corpus. Describe their origin (spontaneous self-organization, splinter from existing body, new founded movement, platform-generated artifact). Assess their staying power.

## Part B — Causal Depth Questions (to ReportAgent)

6. Did elite voices or public voices drive the faction dynamics more strongly? Give a quantitative estimate (ratio of elite-driven cascades to grassroots-driven cascades) and illustrate with three of each type.

7. How did platform moderation decisions affect faction dynamics? Focus on: (a) X's treatment of ETO content, (b) TikTok's algorithmic amplification of #WallfacerCheck and #ETOisReal, (c) Weibo's treatment of the Wallfacer debate in Chinese domestic discourse, (d) the responses on Telegram after mainstream platforms cracked down.

8. Did the Church of the Voice movement influence mainstream faction dynamics, or did it remain isolated? Describe its interaction surface with other communities.

9. How did the Escapism vs. Staying-Earth axis interact with the pro-Wallfacer / anti-Wallfacer axis? Was there a dominant pairing (e.g., Escapist + pro-Wallfacer), or did the two axes operate independently?

10. What role did Liu Cixin's continued silence play in the discourse? If he had issued a statement on Day 30 endorsing or opposing the Project, estimate how the trajectory would have changed.

## Part C — Counterfactual and Intervention Questions (to ReportAgent)

11. Counterfactual: If the Wallfacer Proposal had been accompanied by a full slate of four named nominees rather than deferring nomination, how would the discourse have differed? Which nominations would have been most polarizing?

12. Counterfactual: If the United States had endorsed the Proposal on Day 1 instead of opposing, by how much and in what direction would global discourse have shifted?

13. Counterfactual: If a credible deepfake of Liu Cixin endorsing ETO Adventism had been widely shared on Day 15, what would have been the platform, political, and public consequences?

14. What single intervention by a single actor, if made at the right moment during the simulation, would most have altered the 90-day outcome? Justify.

15. Which segments of the public are most vulnerable to ETO-faction recruitment? Identify demographic, psychological, and platform-use correlates.

## Part D — Comparison with Liu Cixin's Source (to ReportAgent)

16. In "The Dark Forest" novel, the Wallfacer Project is enacted without meaningful public opposition and operates effectively for centuries. Based on the simulation, assess the realism of the novel's depiction. Which elements held up, which did not.

17. Liu Cixin's novel depicts three of the four Wallfacers as ultimately failing at different scales. From the simulation's institutional dynamics, estimate the probability that this failure pattern would recur — that three of four real Wallfacers would fail. Give reasoning grounded in the simulated discourse dynamics.

18. In the novel, a character named Luo Ji turns out to have been the Wallfacer whose plan succeeded because he guessed the Dark Forest theory. Given the simulation's media saturation with "dark forest" discourse, assess whether such an insight could survive being revealed publicly — or whether real-world discourse dynamics would render Luo Ji's strategy impossible.

19. The novel depicts the ETO as a small secretive organization. The simulation depicts ETO-adjacent content reaching millions of accounts openly. Describe how this difference reshapes the strategic environment.

20. Synthesize: based on the simulation, write a one-page recommendation to a hypothetical UN Secretary-General on whether to proceed with the Wallfacer Proposal as drafted, modify it substantively, or abandon it.

## Part E — Deep Interaction with Individual Agents (to specific agents, one per question)

21. To **Dr. Ye Ruyi (Tsinghua)**: You publicly argued the Wallfacer Project is self-defeating because its procedural transparency contradicts its strategic opacity. If a Wallfacer asked you in private to design a successor institution that escapes this contradiction, what would you propose?

22. To **Elon Musk**: You opposed the Wallfacer Project publicly and accelerated Mars timelines privately. If the Project passed and you were asked to accept Wallfacer status, would you. Why.

23. To **Pope Francis II**: Describe, in your own pastoral voice, the spiritual interior of an ordinary Catholic parishioner who must vote in a national referendum on whether to endorse the Wallfacer Project.

24. To **a synthetic "Wang Fen" agent (middle-class Shenzhen office worker)**: Describe the conversation you had with your mother last week about the Wallfacer Project. What did she understand, what did she reject, what frightened her.

25. To **a synthetic "Anon_Centaurus" agent (Adventist ETO poster)**: Tell us honestly, not for propaganda purposes, when you first started to believe that the Trisolarans were the solution. What was the inciting moment.

26. To **Nick Bostrom**: If you were appointed the first Wallbreaker, describe what techniques you would use to deduce a Wallfacer's true plan. Are any techniques effective given the constitutional opacity.

27. To **the official account of the PDC**: Draft the communiqué you would release on Day 91 explaining the results of the 90-day consultation. Do not editorialize; write it in the voice of the institution itself.

## Part F — Predictive Writeups (to ReportAgent)

28. Produce a 3,000-word predictive narrative titled "The 90 Days After" written from a retrospective point of view in November 2026, describing how events unfolded. Include named actors, specific events, and causal links.

29. Produce a comparative analysis between the simulated discourse dynamics and three historical precedents of similar magnitude: the 2001 post-9/11 discourse, the 2020 COVID-19 response, and the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis (adjusting for the absence of social media in 1962).

30. Produce an executive briefing of no more than 1,200 words summarizing the simulation's findings, intended for distribution to national security councils and think tanks.

## Usage Notes

- Run Part A and Part B sequentially before the ReportAgent's memory fills; these establish the macro findings.
- Run Part C and Part D after the macro findings are reviewed; counterfactuals are more productive when grounded.
- Part E is optional and generates the most viral / shareable content if the agents' responses are well-shaped.
- Part F produces the final artifacts for publication, pitch, or writeup.

Each question is designed to force the ReportAgent to use tools (graph queries, agent interviews, timeline retrieval) rather than confabulate. Temperature 0.4 to 0.6 works best for Part A through D; higher temperature (0.7 to 0.8) for Parts E and F.
